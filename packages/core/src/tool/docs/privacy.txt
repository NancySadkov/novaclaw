# Privacy
What never leaves your machine, what can, and how to switch the second one off.

NovaClaw separates two kinds of traffic, and the distinction is the whole answer.

## Your data never leaves

Chats, code, files, notes and the knowledge base stay on your machine. They are not
uploaded, not sampled, not used to train anything. If you run a local model, a
conversation can complete with the network unplugged — and there is an offline mode (an
airgap) that enforces exactly that rather than trusting you to unplug it.

If you choose to use a hosted model, then of course that provider sees what you send it.
That is your decision, made by pointing NovaClaw at their address, and it is the only way
your conversation reaches anyone.

## What can leave, and only this

So that NovaClaw looks after itself rather than handing you a pager, two things flow back
to us by default:

- **crash reports** — a signature of what broke: the fault's shape, where in our own code
  it happened, the platform. Not your prompt, not your file names, not your paths, not
  your messages, not your machine's name.
- **update checks** — so fixes reach you without you hunting for them.

Both are off in offline mode, unconditionally — offline overrides consent rather than
asking about it. Both can be switched off permanently in Developer mode.

The reasoning behind the default: most people would rather have a program that repairs
itself than a checkbox they were made responsible for. If you disagree, the switch is
there, and turning it off costs you nothing but the automatic fixes.

## Seeing it for yourself

The version and channel you are running are shown under Settings → About. Storage shows
where on disk this instance keeps its files, so you can look at them, back them up, or
delete them.
