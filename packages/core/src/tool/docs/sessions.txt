# Chats and sessions
Why a chat behaves like a running program, and what spawning a helper means.

Every chat is a **session**. A session is the unit NovaClaw schedules, lists, suspends and
kills — the same way an operating system treats a running program. That one idea explains
most of the app.

## What a session carries

- the **model** it talks to,
- the **agent** it runs as (its role),
- its **system prompt** (the standing instructions it works under),
- its permissions, and its conversation so far.

## Helper sessions

A session can start another session — a **helper** — to do a piece of the job, then wait
for it and use the result. This is how a big request gets broken into small ones. The
helper is a real chat: you can open it, read it, and see exactly what it was asked and
what it answered. Nothing happens in a hidden place.

The Processes view lists every session with its agent, model, parent and status, and lets
you stop one.

## Settings flow downhill

A helper inherits everything its parent did not explicitly change. Leave a setting alone
and it comes from the parent; set it and it applies from there down.

One setting works differently on purpose: **permissions can only get stricter going
down**. A helper can give up capability it was granted, and can never gain capability its
parent did not have. That is what makes it safe for a session to start helpers at all.

## Ending a session

A session finishes by handing back a result, which its parent then reads. A session you
started yourself just ends when the conversation is over — you can always reopen it.

## Reverting

Each of your messages has a Revert control. It rewinds the conversation to just before
that message, so a wrong turn costs you one click rather than a new chat.
